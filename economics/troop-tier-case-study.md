# Troop tier efficiency: a bounded case study

**Claim:** `TB-ECON-003`  
**Reviewed:** 2026-10-23  
**Status:** conditional historical conclusion requiring publication-grade test evidence.

## Background

A detailed October 2026 Epic combat comparison evaluated whether including additional lower-tier Monster squads created enough attack opportunities to justify their creation and attrition costs. The user studied round ordering, loss costs, targeting, and troop composition rather than simply comparing nominal troop strength.

## Conclusion for that configuration

The analysis led to a decision **not to include M7 squads** in the evaluated Epic attack composition. This is a **configuration-specific resource optimization**, not a claim that M7 should never be used by anyone.

The original analysis involved equipment/leader choices, a particular Epic, and particular unit-cost inputs. Those underlying raw reports and tables are **not published here**; the result should not be cited as a verified public test until supporting evidence is reviewed for sharing.

## Why it may not generalize

Different troop tiers, squad sizes, enemy lineup, buffs, turn order, revival costs, and objective functions can reverse the result. An added tier can generate extra attacks if it survives to act, but can also soak Leadership and impose losses.

## Open experiment

For a fixed Epic state and captain loadout, compare with/without a candidate tier across repeated attacks. Record `XP`, `enemy HP removed`, squad attack counts, troop losses, replacement economics, and variance. Evaluate marginal `(XP gained)/(resource cost added)` and whether the contribution is positive under your chosen objective.
